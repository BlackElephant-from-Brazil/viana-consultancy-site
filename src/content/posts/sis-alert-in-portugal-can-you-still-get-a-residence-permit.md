## SIS Alert and Portugal Residence Permit: A Refusal May Not Be the End

If AIMA has told you that your Portuguese residence permit is blocked because of an alert in the Schengen Information System (SIS), the situation is serious, but an SIS alert does not always mean that obtaining residence in Portugal has become impossible.

Portuguese law lists the absence of an SIS alert among the general conditions for a residence permit. At the same time, when the alert was entered by another EU or Schengen State, AIMA cannot simply see the alert and treat the case as finished.

There is a mandatory consultation procedure that may completely change the legal analysis of your application.

## The question is not simply whether your name appears in SIS

One of the first things we look at in these cases is the administrative file held by AIMA.

Why?

Because the existence of the alert is only one part of the case.

Portuguese law requires AIMA to consult the Member State responsible for certain return or refusal-of-entry alerts before granting or refusing residence. That consultation allows Portugal to understand why the alert exists and to assess its relevance to the residence application.

A recurring problem in the files we review is that this consultation is not properly documented, or appears not to have been carried out at all.

That is not a minor technicality.

In a recent Portuguese administrative court judgment involving this precise issue, the Court found that AIMA could not simply rely on the existence of the SIS indication without first consulting the Member State that had entered it. The omission was considered a breach of the legally required procedure and an instructional defect in the administrative process.

## A SIS alert can exist and Portugal may still grant residence

This is where many applicants misunderstand the system.

The SIS alert cannot simply be ignored. It may represent a genuine obstacle to residence, particularly where public order or security concerns exist.

However, EU law expressly provides a consultation mechanism for situations in which one Member State is considering granting a residence permit to someone who has been entered into SIS by another Member State.

The final decision on whether to grant the residence permit remains with the State considering the residence application.

The Portuguese court decision referred to above recognised exactly this distinction: after obtaining the reasons underlying the alert, AIMA must actually assess them and may reach either a refusal or a grant of residence.

That means an SIS indication should not automatically be treated as the end of the case.

## What we look for in an AIMA SIS case

For us, the key question is usually not:

"Is there an SIS alert?"

It is:

"Was the residence procedure legally conducted after the alert was discovered?"

We normally examine whether:

- the issuing Member State was identified;
- the mandatory consultation took place;
- the reasons behind the alert were obtained;
- AIMA considered those reasons individually;
- the decision contains adequate factual and legal reasoning;
- and the applicant was given a proper opportunity to respond.

If those steps are missing, a refusal may be open to administrative or judicial challenge.

In the judgment we analysed, the Court also found that AIMA had not properly explained how the underlying SIS circumstances could affect public order or national security.

This is precisely why obtaining and analysing the complete administrative file can be more important than simply sending another document to AIMA.

## What happens to the SIS alert if Portugal grants residence?

Depending on the type of alert, EU rules provide mechanisms requiring the Member State that entered it to delete the relevant SIS indication where Portugal decides to grant or maintain a residence permit.

Individuals also have rights to access the personal data recorded about them in SIS and, where appropriate, request correction or deletion of inaccurate or unlawfully stored information.

The exact strategy therefore depends on the type of SIS alert, the country that issued it and what has already happened in the Portuguese residence procedure.
