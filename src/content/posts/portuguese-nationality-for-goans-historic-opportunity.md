## Legal Basis: Who Qualifies for Portuguese Nationality?

The right to Portuguese nationality is grounded in Portuguese law, which recognizes the rights of individuals born in the former Portuguese territories of India until December 19, 1961, the date Goa, Daman, and Diu were annexed by the Republic of India.

**Eligible individuals include:**

1. **Those born in Goa, Daman, or Diu before December 19, 1961:**
   - Under Portuguese rule, these individuals were recognized as full Portuguese citizens.
   - This status remains valid under Portuguese law to this day.
2. **Direct descendants (children and grandchildren):**
   - Children and grandchildren of individuals born in Goa before 1961 are also eligible to apply for Portuguese nationality.
   - Proof of direct lineage is required, and each generation must legally validate their nationality before it can be passed on to the next generation.

## Limitations for Great-Grandchildren

Portuguese law does not automatically grant nationality to great-grandchildren unless previous generations have formally registered their Portuguese nationality. In simpler terms, the right to Portuguese nationality must be transmitted sequentially across generations, and each generation must apply individually.

## Essential Process and Documentation

The process of obtaining Portuguese nationality demands precision and adherence to legal requirements. The core documents required to initiate the process include:

1. Birth certificate (issued during the colonial period).
2. Documents of parents or grandparents (to prove direct descent).
3. Proof of connection to Goa, Daman, or Diu prior to December 1961 (e.g., historical or religious records).
4. Valid identification documents (passport or identification card).

In cases where such records are unavailable, we can facilitate thorough research in historical archives in both Portugal and Goa to retrieve essential documentation.
